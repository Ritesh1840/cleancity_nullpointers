A practical guide to handling waste

What goes where, what to avoid, and how to dispose of the awkward things.

Wet and dry waste

Separating waste at home is the most useful single step. Mixed waste is hard to recycle and ends up in landfill.

Wet

  • Food scraps
  • Vegetable peels
  • Garden leaves

Dry

  • Paper, cardboard
  • Plastic, metal, glass
  • Clean packaging

Hazardous

  • Batteries, bulbs
  • Medicines
  • Paint, chemicals

Reducing plastic

Most plastic litter starts as single-use convenience items. Replace them where you can.

  • Carry a cloth bag and a refillable bottle.
  • Refuse plastic cutlery and straws with takeaway food.
  • Buy in bulk to cut down on small packets.

Recycling guidelines

  • Empty and rinse bottles, cans and jars.
  • Flatten cardboard boxes.
  • Keep food-soiled paper out of the dry bin.
  • Check what your local collector accepts.

E-waste disposal

Phones, chargers and batteries contain metals that leak into soil and water when dumped.

Never put batteries or electronics in the regular bin. Hand them to an authorised e-waste collector or a manufacturer take-back point.

Responsible disposal

  • Put waste out only at your area's collection time.
  • Keep bin lids closed and do not overfill them.
  • Arrange proper pick-up for construction debris.
